## Abstract
Preclinical rodent models—encompassing genetically engineered, chemically induced, and surgically established systems—are indispensable for dissecting the pathobiology of human diseases and evaluating therapeutic efficacy. However, reliance on unimodal molecular assays or unstandardized tissue assessments frequently fails to capture the complex spatial, structural, and functional manifestations of disease progression, contributing to a persistent translational gap between preclinical findings and clinical outcomes. Integrating high-specificity histopathology, longitudinal *in vivo* imaging, complementary *ex vivo* three-dimensional (3D) tissue analysis, and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted quantitative image analysis offers a comprehensive framework for deep morphological phenotyping.

This structured thematic literature synthesis integrates methodological protocols, quantitative meta-analytic findings, and experimental standardization frameworks across eight peer-reviewed review-based publications and one original experimental study incorporating systematic review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation. Collectively, these nine publications span neurodegenerative (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and spinal muscular atrophy), cerebrovascular (Alzheimer’s disease-related vascular dysfunction and ischemic stroke), sensorineural (auditory and vestibular disorders), inflammatory (allergic rhinitis and medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw), and neoplastic (oral epithelial dysplasia and colorectal carcinogenesis) rodent models, with murine models serving as the primary experimental focus supplemented by comparative rat, hamster, and human tissue validation evidence. Specifically, this synthesis examines how motor-neuron-associated markers, including choline acetyltransferase (`ChAT`) and `HB9`, can improve cellular identification compared with less specific conventional staining approaches, while vascular basement membrane and tight-junction markers provide complementary information on neurovascular integrity. Furthermore, it evaluates the integration of magnetic resonance imaging (`DSC-MRI`, `ASL-MRI`, `DCE-MRI` $K^{\text{trans}}$, `DWI/ADC`), positron emission tomography (`FDG-PET`), and optical tissue clearing with deep learning-based automated cellular segmentation to support spatially resolved, quantitative characterization of cellular, vascular, and tissue-level phenotypes across complementary imaging modalities. Finally, critical methodological bottlenecks—including anatomical sampling variability, induction dose–duration trade-offs, sex bias, and incomplete adherence to **ARRIVE 2.0** and risk-of-bias reporting standards—are appraised to establish an actionable framework for translational preclinical phenotyping.

**Keywords:** Deep phenotyping; Histopathology; Multimodal imaging; Deep learning segmentation; Preclinical rodent models; Experimental reproducibility; Tissue clearing

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Preclinical Rodent Models in Translational Biomedical Research
Chronic, progressive, and multifactorial human diseases—spanning neurodegenerative disorders, cerebrovascular accidents, sensorineural deficits, chronic mucosal inflammation, and malignancies—impose severe clinical and socioeconomic burdens globally (Agnello & Ciaccio, 2022; Corcia et al., 2025; Feigin et al., 2021; Bray et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026). Although human clinical and postmortem specimens provide essential translational evidence, their use is constrained by factors such as limited tissue availability, invasive sampling requirements, postmortem tissue degradation, and substantial inter-individual genetic and clinical heterogeneity. Consequently, preclinical rodent models—principally *Mus musculus*, complemented by *Rattus norvegicus* and *Mesocricetus auratus*—remain indispensable for investigating longitudinal disease mechanisms and therapeutic interventions under controlled experimental conditions (Lewandowski et al., 2020; Vandamme, 2014; Stifani, 2014; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

Genetically engineered murine models enable precise interrogation of human disease alleles along a continuum from molecular perturbation to tissue-level pathology. For example, humanized Apolipoprotein E4 (`APOE4`) targeted-replacement (`APOE-TR`) and knock-in (`APOE-KI`) mouse lines—as well as compound `APOE-TR;5xFAD` (`EFAD`) models—allow researchers to dissect isoform-specific cerebrovascular pathology and blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction in Alzheimer’s disease (Montagne et al., 2020, 2021; Tai et al., 2017; Laing et al., 2026). Similarly, large-scale knockout mouse phenotyping initiatives, such as the **International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium (IMPC)**, systematically evaluate physiological and sensory phenotypes across thousands of gene-targeted lines to resolve the genetic architecture of hereditary sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) and vestibular dysfunction (Dickinson et al., 2016; Muñoz-Fuentes et al., 2018; Ayoub et al., 2025). Complementing genetic mouse models, chemically and surgically induced rodent paradigms—including 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (`DMH`)-induced colorectal carcinogenesis (Venkatachalam et al., 2020; Clapper et al., 2020; Salehi et al., 2026), 4-nitroquinoline-1-oxide (`4-NQO`)- and 7,12-dimethylbenz[*a*]anthracene (`DMBA`)-induced oral epithelial dysplasia (`OED`) (Tang et al., 2004; Chen et al., 2018; Ngokwe et al., 2026), ovalbumin (`OVA`) plus aluminum hydroxide ($\text{Al(OH)}_3$)-sensitized allergic rhinitis (He et al., 2015; Zeng & Zhou, 2023; Chen et al., 2026), bisphosphonate-induced medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaws (`MRONJ`) (Aguirre et al., 2021; Yan et al., 2022; Maciel et al., 2026), and transient intraluminal filament middle cerebral artery occlusion (`tMCAO`) for focal ischemic stroke (Koizumi et al., 1986; Longa et al., 1989; Mendes et al., 2026)—recapitulate multistage tissue injury and pathological progression.

### 1.2 The Translational Bottleneck and Limitations of Unimodal Assessment
Despite substantial advances in preclinical biomedical research, a persistent translational gap continues to hinder the development of effective therapies: numerous candidate neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and antineoplastic agents that demonstrate efficacy in rodent studies fail to translate into clinical benefit in human trials (Sutherland et al., 2012; Mirzayans & Murray, 2022; Mendes et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026). A major contributor to this translational attrition is the reliance on fragmented, unimodal, or poorly standardized phenotypic readouts that fail to capture the spatial, structural, and temporal complexity of human pathology (Fisher et al., 2009; Dirnagl, 2006; Mendes et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

At the cellular and histopathological levels, conventional staining protocols—such as hematoxylin and eosin (`H&E`), Nissl staining, and single-marker immunohistochemistry—frequently suffer from limited cellular specificity and two-dimensional (2D) sampling bias (Ferrucci et al., 2018; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). In spinal cord neurodegeneration models of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (`ALS`) and spinal muscular atrophy (`SMA`), conventional Nissl and non-phosphorylated neurofilament (`SMI-32`) staining may fail to distinguish motor neurons reliably from other ventral horn neuronal populations; indeed, Sowoidnich et al. (2026) identified substantial variability in reported motor neuron loss across SMA mouse studies, with anatomical sampling region emerging as a major methodological contributor (Bowerman et al., 2010; Tsang et al., 2000; Chalif et al., 2022). Although terminal endpoints such as 2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride (`TTC`) staining and wet/dry brain water quantification provide useful measures of tissue injury and edema, they offer endpoint-specific assessments rather than continuous longitudinal characterization of disease progression (Benedek et al., 2006; Türeyen et al., 2004; Mendes et al., 2026). Furthermore, conventional 2D *in vitro* cultures and uncalibrated animal models often omit critical tumor–stromal microenvironments, vascular basement membrane dynamics, and sex- or age-dependent physiological modifiers (Ferreira et al., 2018; Abuwatfa et al., 2024; Laing et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026).

### 1.3 Convergence of High-Specificity Histopathology, Multimodal Imaging, and AI
To overcome the limitations of isolated molecular or histological readouts, contemporary biomedical research is advancing along a unified analytical trajectory:

Achieving rigorous deep phenotyping across intact organs requires three synergistic pillars:

1. **Cell-Type-Specific Molecular Histology & 3D Tissue Clearing:** Replacing non-specific stains with lineage-restricted markers—such as choline acetyltransferase (`ChAT`) and the homeobox transcription factor `HB9` for spinal motor neurons (Borges & Iversen, 1986; Sowoidnich et al., 2026), or neurovascular markers representing distinct structural compartments, including `CD13` for pericyte-associated identification, collagen IV and laminin for vascular basement membrane integrity, and occludin for endothelial tight-junction organization (Nishitsuji et al., 2011; Yamazaki et al., 2020; Laing et al., 2026)—substantially improves cellular classification. When combined with suitable labeling or tracing strategies, optical tissue-clearing approaches, including refractive-index matching with `RapiClear®`, can facilitate three-dimensional visualization of selected neural and vascular structures while reducing the need for serial physical sectioning. However, clearing efficiency, tissue preservation, and imaging quality remain dependent on tissue type and protocol-specific conditions (Buettner et al., 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
2. **Longitudinal Multimodal *In Vivo* Imaging:** Non-invasive *in vivo* modalities, including `ASL-MRI`, `DSC-MRI`, `DCE-MRI`, `DWI/ADC` mapping, and $^{18}\text{F}$-`FDG-PET`, provide complementary information on cerebral perfusion, vascular permeability, tissue diffusion, and glucose metabolism. By contrast, $^{14}\text{C}$-iodoantipyrine autoradiography provides a terminal, *ex vivo* measure of cerebral blood flow and should be distinguished from longitudinal imaging approaches (Koizumi et al., 2018; Montagne et al., 2021; Kuo et al., 2024; Onos et al., 2024; Laing et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026). Supplementary methodological literature further highlights complementary modalities such as micro-computed tomography (`micro-CT`), optical coherence tomography (`OCT`), and Raman spectroscopy for resolving bone microarchitecture and early epithelial dysplasia (Biguetti et al., 2019; Smith et al., 2011; Mian et al., 2017; Maciel et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026).
3. **AI-Driven Quantitative Image Analysis & Experimental Standardization:** As 3D confocal/light-sheet microscopy and multimodal imaging generate massive volumetric datasets, manual cell counting and region-of-interest tracing become major bottlenecks prone to inter-observer variability (Ferrucci et al., 2018; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Generalist deep-learning-based cellular segmentation architectures can facilitate automated quantification and reduce manual counting workload and observer-dependent variability. However, their reliability depends on image quality, tissue-specific model adaptation, expert-annotated training data, and independent validation across experimental conditions (Stringer et al., 2021; Pachitariu & Stringer, 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Simultaneously, rigorous adherence to preclinical reporting guidelines—such as **ARRIVE 2.0** (Percie du Sert et al., 2020; Mendes et al., 2026; Salehi et al., 2026)—and risk-of-bias frameworks such as **SYRCLE’s Risk of Bias** tool (Hooijmans et al., 2014; Hu et al., 2026; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026) is essential to control confounders such as anatomical sampling bias, induction dose–duration variability, and anesthetic effects.

### 1.4 Scope and Objectives of This Synthesis
This structured thematic literature synthesis integrates methodological designs, quantitative meta-analytic outcomes, and reported quality appraisal metrics across eight review-based publications and one original experimental study incorporating systematic review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation (Hu et al., 2026; Ayoub et al., 2025; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026; Salehi et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Specifically, this work aims to:
1. Evaluate how genetic, chemical, and surgical rodent models recapitulate organ-specific human pathobiology across neurodegenerative, cerebrovascular, sensorineural, inflammatory, and neoplastic conditions, distinguishing primary murine findings from complementary rat, hamster, and human tissue validation evidence.
2. Compare classical histopathological endpoints against lineage-specific molecular markers and multimodal *in vivo*/*ex vivo* imaging techniques for quantifying structural and functional phenotypes.
3. Examine the application of deep learning-based automated cellular segmentation and computational simulation in resolving anatomical sampling bias and inter-study heterogeneity.
4. Synthesize critical experimental confounders—including induction protocols, sex bias, and risk-of-bias deficits—to outline standardized best practices for reproducible preclinical phenotyping.

## 2. Methodology & Analytical Framework

### 2.1 Synthesis Design, Unit of Analysis, and Publication Scope
This work is conducted as a structured thematic literature synthesis rather than a *de novo* systematic review or primary meta-analysis. The primary unit of analysis comprises nine peer-reviewed publications: eight review-based publications, including systematic reviews, systematic reviews with meta-analysis, and one systematic meta-review (Hu et al., 2026; Ayoub et al., 2025; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026; Ngokwe et al., 2026; Salehi et al., 2026), and one original experimental research article that integrates a systematic literature review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

Publications were purposively curated through targeted screening of recent peer-reviewed biomedical literature to construct a cohesive methodological progression linking molecular and computational disease biology $\rightarrow$ tissue-level pathobiology $\rightarrow$ morphological phenotyping $\rightarrow$ multimodal imaging $\rightarrow$ AI-assisted quantitative analysis. A tenth candidate publication examining ionizing radiation-induced bone microarchitecture was excluded during final scope refinement to maintain thematic focus on cellular, neurovascular, mucosal, and neoplastic disease models. To preserve strict source traceability across secondary syntheses and underlying primary investigations, all pooled effect sizes, study counts, and risk-of-bias scores are explicitly attributed to the parent publications and their cited primary experiments.

### 2.2 Information Sources and Protocol Transparency Across Included Studies
Within the nine synthesized publications, primary literature searches were executed across major biomedical, multidisciplinary, and multi-omic databases, including MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane Library, LILACS, CNKI, Google Scholar, and specialized phenotyping repositories such as the International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium (IMPC), Deafness Variation Database (DVD), Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM), and the gEAR gene expression portal.

Six of the included systematic reviews pre-registered their protocols on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (**PROSPERO**) or the Open Science Framework (**OSF**), and seven explicitly adhered to the **PRISMA / PRISMA 2020** guidelines (Page et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2026 `[CRD420251229183]`; Ayoub et al., 2025 `[CRD420251029358]`; Laing et al., 2026 `[CRD420251010665 & SearchRxiv DOI: 10.1079/searchRxiv.2025.01124]`; Maciel et al., 2026 `[CRD42024571152]`; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026 `[CRD420251140869 & OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/F4EPQ]`; Ngokwe et al., 2026 `[CRD42025605982]`; Salehi et al., 2026).

### 2.3 Species Stratification and Preclinical Model Classification
Because translational validity depends heavily on species-specific anatomy, physiology, and genetic background, evidence across the nine publications was stratified into primary murine models and complementary non-murine rodent and human validation datasets:

1. **Primary Murine Models (*Mus musculus*):**
   * **Neurodegenerative Transgenic Lines:** Transgenic `SOD1-G93A` mice on C57BL/6 or B6SJL backgrounds for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (`ALS`) (Hu et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026 `[JAX002726]`), alongside severe `SMNΔ7` ($\text{Smn}^{+/-}; \text{SMN2}^{+/+}; \text{SMN}\Delta 7^{+/+}$ on FVB background, `JAX005025`), mild 4-copy `SMN2` Type III-like spinal muscular atrophy (`SMA`; backcrossed onto C57BL/6N), $\text{Smn}^{2B/-}$ Taiwanese SMA lines, and `ChAT-eGFP` reporter mice (`JAX007902`) (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
   * **Humanized Knock-In & Targeted-Replacement Lines:** Humanized `APOE3-TR` and `APOE4-TR` mice (on C57BL/6J or C57BL/6N backgrounds) and compound `APOE-TR;5xFAD` (`E3FAD` and `E4FAD`) models for Alzheimer’s disease-related cerebrovascular dysfunction (Montagne et al., 2020; Tai et al., 2017; Barisano et al., 2022; Laing et al., 2026).
   * **High-Throughput Knockout Pipelines:** A total of $3,006$ gene-knockout mouse strains systematically screened by the IMPC for auditory brainstem response (`ABR`) thresholds and vestibular phenotypes (Dickinson et al., 2016; Bowl et al., 2017; Ayoub et al., 2025).
   * **Immunological Sensitization Strains:** Wild-type BALB/c mice (accounting for $85.33\%$ of included studies), alongside C57BL/6, ICR, 129/Sv, and Kunming mice, sensitized with ovalbumin (`OVA`) and aluminum hydroxide ($\text{Al(OH)}_3$) adjuvant for allergic rhinitis (Chen et al., 2026).
2. **Complementary Rodent & Human Validation Evidence (*Rattus norvegicus*, *Mesocricetus auratus*, and *Homo sapiens*):**
   * **Surgical Focal Ischemia (Rat):** Adult Sprague–Dawley and Wistar rats subjected to transient intraluminal filament middle cerebral artery occlusion (`tifMCAO`) (Koizumi et al., 1986; Longa et al., 1989; Mendes et al., 2026).
   * **Pharmacological & Trauma-Induced Osteonecrosis (Rat & Mouse):** Rats (Wistar, Sprague–Dawley, Rice rats) and mice (C57BL/6J, BALB/c, 129S/v) treated with bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid [`ZOL`] or alendronate) combined with tooth extraction, alveolar socket regularization, local infection, or immunosuppressants (dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide) for medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaws (`MRONJ`) (Biguetti et al., 2019; Maciel et al., 2026).
   * **Chemical Carcinogenesis Models (Rat, Mouse & Hamster):** Rats, mice, and Syrian golden hamsters exposed to 4-nitroquinoline-1-oxide (`4-NQO`) or 7,12-dimethylbenz[*a*]anthracene (`DMBA`) for oral epithelial dysplasia (`OED`) (Tang et al., 2004; Ngokwe et al., 2026), and Wistar or Sprague–Dawley rats administered 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (`DMH`) for multistage colorectal carcinogenesis (Venkatachalam et al., 2020; Salehi et al., 2026).
   * **Human Clinical & Postmortem Validation:** Human sensorineural hearing-loss cohorts from the Deafness Variation Database (Ayoub et al., 2025) and human postmortem thoracic spinal cord tissue from SMA patients and age-matched controls (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

### 2.4 Thematic Evidence Extraction and Reported Quality Appraisal
Variables and methodological parameters were extracted from the nine publications across four thematic domains:

* **Domain 1 — Histopathological & Molecular Phenotyping:** Cellular marker specificity (`ChAT`, `HB9`, `NeuN`, `SMI-32`, Nissl, `H&E`, `TTC`, Cresyl Violet), stereological and Abercrombie nuclear-splitting corrections:
  $$N = \frac{n \times T}{T + D}$$
  (Sowoidnich et al., 2026), vascular basement membrane and tight-junction proteins (collagen-IV, laminin, `CD31`, `CD13`, occludin, claudin-5, `ZO-1`, `MMP-2`, `MMP-9`, fibrinogen, fibronectin) (Laing et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026), neuroinflammatory and apoptotic signaling mediators (`Iba-1`, `GFAP`, `TLR4`, `NF-κB`, `p-p38`, `TNF-α`, `IL-1β`, `IL-4`, `IL-5`, `IL-6`, `p-Akt`, `p-GSK-3β`, `RhoA`, `TDP-43`, `p53`) (Hu et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026), histological bone necrosis (Maciel et al., 2026), OED severity grades (Ngokwe et al., 2026), and a four-grade colonic lesion scale (Grade 1: normal; Grade 2: aberrant crypt foci [`ACF`] only; Grade 3: tumor only; Grade 4: both ACF and tumor) (Salehi et al., 2026).
* **Domain 2 — Whole-Mount Optical Clearing & Multimodal Imaging:** *Ex vivo* 3D optical tissue clearing protocols—specifically anterior corpectomy dissection, transcardial $4\%$ paraformaldehyde perfusion, retrograde ventral root filling with Fluorescein Dextran and Texas Red, and refractive-index matching in `RapiClear® 1.49` for $24\ \text{h}$ at $37\ ^\circ\text{C}$ prior to $25\times$ confocal z-stack imaging at $1\ \mu\text{m}$ step size (Sowoidnich et al., 2026)—alongside *in vivo* neuroimaging modalities, including `ASL-MRI`, `DSC-MRI`, `DCE-MRI` ($K^{\text{trans}}$), $T_2$-weighted MRI, `DWI/PWI`, `ADC` mapping, $^{14}\text{C}$-iodoantipyrine autoradiography, and $^{18}\text{F}$-`FDG-PET` (Laing et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026).
* **Domain 3 — AI-Driven Image Segmentation & Computational Simulation:** In 3D whole-mount spinal cord datasets, confocal z-stacks were preprocessed in **FIJI (ImageJ)** (despeckling, median filtering at $0.3$-pixel radius, rolling-ball background subtraction at $60$-pixel radius, and histogram equalization) and segmented using the open-source deep learning algorithm **Cellpose** (fine-tuned from the `TissueNet` base model via human-in-the-loop annotation using `ChAT` somatic and `HB9` nuclear signals) (Stringer et al., 2021; Pachitariu & Stringer, 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Automated enumeration agreement was benchmarked against manual ground-truth counts using a study-defined adapted counting-agreement metric:
  $$\text{Counting Agreement} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}$$
  alongside R-based randomization scripts (`Counting-Simulation`) evaluating anatomical subsampling strategies (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
* **Domain 4 — Quantitative Synthesis & Reported Risk-of-Bias Metrics:** All meta-analytic estimates, meta-regressions, and methodological quality scores reported in Section 3 represent secondary syntheses of the statistical analyses performed by the original review authors (using R `meta`/`metafor`, Stata, SPSS, GraphPad Prism, WebPlotDigitizer, and GetData Graph Digitizer) (Drevon et al., 2017; Hu et al., 2026; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Salehi et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Similarly, internal validity and reporting quality ratings—evaluated via **SYRCLE’s Risk of Bias** tool (Hooijmans et al., 2014), the **CAMARADES** checklist (Macleod et al., 2004), **ARRIVE Guidelines 2.0** (Percie du Sert et al., 2020), **OHAT**, **ROBINS-E**, and adapted **GRADE**—reflect the published assessments reported within the respective source reviews rather than independent re-scoring.

## 3. Results & Thematic Synthesis

Across the eight review-based publications and one original experimental study incorporating systematic review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation, empirical evidence converges around four analytical dimensions of deep morphological phenotyping: (1) tissue-level pathobiology across genetic and induced models, (2) histopathological marker specificity and multimodal imaging, (3) AI-driven automated cellular quantification, and (4) experimental standardization and reproducibility.

### 3.1 Tissue-Level Pathobiology Across Preclinical Disease Models

#### 3.1.1 Neurodegenerative Motor Neuron Protection and Interspecies Sensory Genetics
* **Multimodal Neuroprotection in ALS (`SOD1-G93A` Mice):** Synthesizing 17 preclinical studies ($n = 372$ animals; mean reported CAMARADES quality score $7/10$), Hu et al. (2026) evaluated functional and mechanistic endpoints of electroacupuncture (`EA`) in transgenic `SOD1-G93A` mice:
  * *Primary Functional & Survival Outcomes:* `EA` significantly improved primary behavioral motor coordination on the rotarod test ($\text{SMD} = 3.31$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [2.05, 4.57]$, $p = 0.001$, $I^2 = 88.6\%$; directionally robust following trim-and-fill adjustment), increased open-field locomotion ($\text{MD} = 1036.74\ \text{cm}$, $p < 0.001$), delayed disease onset ($\text{MD} = 14.46\ \text{days}$, $p < 0.001$), and extended overall survival ($\text{MD} = 13.43\ \text{days}$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [2.32, 24.54]$, $p = 0.018$) (Hu et al., 2026).
  * *Secondary Histopathological & Molecular Outcomes:* At the tissue level, `EA` preserved lumbar anterior horn motor neuron counts ($\text{MD} = 6.98$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [5.67, 8.29]$, $Z = 10.448$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 0\%$) (Wang et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2017; Hu et al., 2026). Mechanistically, this structural protection corresponded with suppression of microglial neuroinflammation—evidenced by reduced `Iba-1` ($\text{SMD} = -6.95$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [-10.53, -3.37]$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 84.9\%$), `TLR4` ($\text{MD} = -0.61$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 0\%$), `NF-κB` ($\text{SMD} = -5.74$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 1.5\%$), phosphorylated `p38` ($\text{MD} = -0.20$, $p < 0.001$), `TNF-α` ($\text{SMD} = -5.76$, $p < 0.001$), `IL-6` ($\text{SMD} = -5.49$, $p = 0.004$), and `IL-1β` ($\text{SMD} = -7.75$, $p < 0.001$) (Jiang et al., 2011; Yang et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2026)—alongside enhanced pro-survival `p-Akt` ($\text{SMD} = 4.93$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 0\%$) and inhibitory `p-GSK-3β` ($\text{MD} = 0.48$, $p = 0.002$), reduced `RhoA`-mediated axonal growth inhibition ($\text{MD} = -0.32$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 0\%$), and decreased cytoplasmic `TDP-43` aggregation ($\text{MD} = -0.23$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 48.9\%$) (Sun et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2026).
* **Interspecies Auditory and Vestibular Phenotyping:** Cross-referencing 454 studies (433 human, 15 mouse, 6 interspecies) alongside the IMPC ($3,006$ knockout strains) and Deafness Variation Database (DVD), Ayoub et al. (2025) identified $331$ murine and $222$ human hearing-loss genes, with $31$ interspecies genes shared across both repositories (Bowl et al., 2017; Ayoub et al., 2025). Transcriptomic integration via the **gEAR** portal showed that $25$ of these $31$ interspecies genes are co-expressed across postnatal (`P0–P1`) cochlear and vestibular hair cells and non-hair cells. Five genes—`CIB2`, `COL9A2`, `CLRN1`, `GRXCR1`, and `MARVELD2`—met strict interspecies audiovestibular criteria, while combined phenotypic and expression profiling prioritized `NIN`, `CCDC88C`, `OTOG`, `CLRN1`, `GRXCR1`, and `MARVELD2` as candidate genes for Meniere’s disease (Goodyear et al., 2012; Odeh et al., 2010; Ayoub et al., 2025). Protein–protein interaction (`PPI`) mapping in *Homo sapiens* (83 nodes, 178 edges) and *Mus musculus* revealed a conserved core interactome centered on stereocilia mechanotransduction hubs (`MYO6`, `MYO3A`, `CIB2`, `USH1C`, `GRXCR1`) and tectorial/otolithic extracellular matrix clusters (`COL9A2`, `COL9A3`, `OTOG`, `OTOGL`, `OTOA`) (Ayoub et al., 2025).

#### 3.1.2 Cerebrovascular Remodelling and Blood–Brain Barrier (BBB) Breakdown in APOE4 Mice
Synthesizing 18 studies evaluating humanized `APOE3-TR`, `APOE4-TR`, and `EFAD` mice (mean reported CAMARADES score $7.2/10$), Laing et al. (2026) established that the `APOE4` allele is associated with progressive BBB destabilization across three converging mechanistic axes (Bell et al., 2012; Montagne et al., 2020, 2021; Laing et al., 2026):

1. **Cyclophilin A (`CypA`)–`NF-κB`–`MMP9` Inflammatory Axis:** `APOE4` mice exhibit a 5- to 6-fold elevation in pericytic `CypA` (Bell et al., 2012) and a $56\%$ increase in cortical endothelial `MMP9` immunoreactivity (Laing et al., 2026), triggering enzymatic degradation of basement membrane collagen-IV and focal tight-junction membrane gaps under transmission electron microscopy alongside reduced astrocytic end-foot coverage (Yamazaki et al., 2020; Laing et al., 2026).
2. **Occludin Phosphorylation & Extracellular Matrix (ECM) Instability:** Impaired `LRP1`–`PKCη` signaling reduces threonine phosphorylation of occludin, lowering trans-endothelial electrical resistance (`TEER`) (Nishitsuji et al., 2011; Laing et al., 2026). *In vivo*, progressive loss of `CD13+` pericyte coverage inversely correlates with pericapillary fibrinogen accumulation (elevated by $\sim 65\%$ in 8-month-old female `E4FAD` mice) and fibronectin deposition (Barisano et al., 2022; Laing et al., 2026). In random-effects meta-analysis ($k = 6$ studies), the pooled estimate suggested a directionally lower expression of immunohistochemical vascular morphological markers (laminin, collagen-IV, `CD31`) in `APOE4` compared with `APOE3` mice ($\text{SMD} = -0.59$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [-1.39, 0.20]$, $I^2 = 66.3\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.64$); however, because the confidence interval spanned zero ($p = 0.14$), this effect represents a non-significant directional trend rather than a statistically confirmed difference (Laing et al., 2026).
3. **Insulin/mTOR Metabolic Vulnerability:** `APOE4` induces endothelial insulin resistance and maladaptive `mTOR` hyperactivity, suppressing P-glycoprotein (`P-gp`) efflux transport ($p < 0.001$) and reducing cerebral glucose uptake on longitudinal $^{18}\text{F}$-`FDG-PET`—deficits rescued by 16 weeks of rapamycin administration (Onos et al., 2024; Laing et al., 2026).

#### 3.1.3 Maxillofacial, Mucosal, and Multistage Neoplastic Pathology
* **Medication-Related Osteonecrosis of the Jaws (`MRONJ`):** Across 119 rodent studies (105 in quantitative meta-analysis; 84 rat, 21 mouse), Maciel et al. (2026) reported pooled histological osteonecrosis incidences of $28.60\%$ in rats ($95\%\ \text{CI}\ [20.24\%, 37.61\%]$, $I^2 = 90.3\%$) and $23.81\%$ in mice ($95\%\ \text{CI}\ [8.24\%, 43.05\%]$, $I^2 = 88.6\%$), whereas macroscopic oral bone exposure was substantially lower at $19.96\%$ in rats and $8.6\%$ in mice (Biguetti et al., 2019; Maciel et al., 2026). Multivariate meta-regression demonstrated that induction protocol and cumulative zoledronic acid (`ZOL`) dose accounted for $75.42\%$ of between-study heterogeneity in rats ($p < 0.0001$) and $77.78\%$ in mice ($p < 0.0001$), with peak histological osteonecrosis observed in `ZOL` + tooth extraction + alveolar socket regularization in rats ($93.44\%$) and `ZOL` + dexamethasone + extraction in mice ($78.85\%$) (Maciel et al., 2026).
* **Allergic Rhinitis Mucosal Histopathology:** Synthesizing 75 mouse studies (64 BALB/c studies in primary meta-analysis), Chen et al. (2026) demonstrated that ovalbumin (`OVA`) combined with aluminum hydroxide ($\text{Al(OH)}_3$) induces robust Th2-skewed nasal inflammation, characterized by marked nasal mucosal eosinophil infiltration ($\text{SMD} = 6.58$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [5.47, 7.69]$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 87.4\%$), elevated serum OVA-specific `IgE` ($\text{SMD} = 8.53$, $p < 0.001$, $I^2 = 91.5\%$), and increased `IL-4` ($\text{SMD} = 5.45$) and `IL-5` ($\text{SMD} = 4.56$) in nasal lavage fluid (He et al., 2015; Zeng & Zhou, 2023; Chen et al., 2026).
* **Multistage Oral and Colorectal Carcinogenesis:** Ngokwe et al. (2026) mapped 307 models of oral epithelial dysplasia (`OED`) across 292 studies ($88.9\%$ *in vivo* vs. $11.1\%$ *in vitro*, with $73.5\%$ of *in vitro* models restricted to 2D cultures), showing that `4-NQO` predominates in rats ($92.3\%$) and mice ($85\%$), whereas `DMBA` predominates in hamsters ($90.6\%$) (Tang et al., 2004; Ngokwe et al., 2026). In colorectal cancer, Salehi et al. (2026) analyzed 430 rat `DMH` studies and established a clear dose–duration staging relationship: high-dose/short-duration regimens ($80\ \text{mg/kg/week}$ s.c. for 6 weeks) achieved $92\%$ target induction of preneoplastic aberrant crypt foci (`ACF`; mean incidence $85\%$), whereas moderate-dose/long-duration regimens ($40\ \text{mg/kg/week}$ s.c. for 12 weeks) achieved $88\%$ target induction of combined `ACF` and invasive adenocarcinomas (mean tumor incidence $65\%$) (Venkatachalam et al., 2020; Clapper et al., 2020; Salehi et al., 2026).

### 3.2 Histopathological Marker Specificity and Multimodal Imaging Readouts

#### 3.2.1 Resolving Off-Target Cellular Labelling in Histopathology
Sowoidnich et al. (2026) provided quantitative experimental evidence that classical histological stains severely compromise cellular phenotyping accuracy in the murine spinal cord:
* **Interneuron Misclassification by `SMI-32` and Nissl:** Quadruple immunolabeling (`ChAT`, `HB9`, `SMI-32`, Nissl) in `P42` C57BL/6 mice revealed that while `ChAT` and `HB9` selectively label true $\alpha$- and $\gamma$-motor neurons, `SMI-32` and Nissl exhibit extensive off-target binding to ventral horn interneurons—specifically ventral spinocerebellar tract (`VSCT`) neurons, calbindin+ Renshaw cells, and `FoxP2+` V1 interneurons—detecting $\sim 160\%$ (`SMI-32`) and $\sim 280\%$ (Nissl) additional non-motor neurons in segment L1 and $\sim 75\%$ and $\sim 120\%$ in segment L5 (Borges & Iversen, 1986; Tsang et al., 2000; Chalif et al., 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
* **Failure of Soma-Size Filtering & Fixation Sensitivity:** Cross-sectional soma morphometry demonstrated that `ChAT-` off-target profiles labeled by `SMI-32` or Nissl have mean areas of $\sim 275\text{–}300\ \mu\text{m}^2$, overlapping directly with the smaller $\gamma$-motor neuron distribution (`ChAT+` bimodal peaks at $\sim 232\ \mu\text{m}^2$ and $\sim 440\text{–}490\ \mu\text{m}^2$), proving that size gating cannot prevent false-positive counts (Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Furthermore, transcardial $4\%$ PFA perfusion is mandatory to preserve cytoplasmic `ChAT` immunoreactivity in adult myelinated tissue (`P60`), whereas immersion fixation rapidly degrades `ChAT` signal (Sowoidnich et al., 2026). Thick vibratome sections ($75\ \mu\text{m}$) also minimize nuclear-splitting artifacts, requiring only a $\sim 10\%$ Abercrombie correction compared to $\sim 35\%$ in thin cryostat sections ($20\ \mu\text{m}$) (Ferrucci et al., 2018; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).


#### 3.2.2 Multimodal *In Vivo* Neuroimaging and *Ex Vivo* 3D Tissue Clearing
* **Cerebral Blood Flow & BBB Permeability Imaging (`APOE4` & Ischemic Stroke):** Hierarchical robust variance estimation (`RVE`) meta-analysis across `DSC-MRI`, `ASL-MRI`, and $^{14}\text{C}$-iodoantipyrine autoradiography studies confirmed a statistically significant reduction in cerebral blood flow (`CBF`) in `APOE4` relative to `APOE3` mice ($\text{SMD} = -2.87$, $95\%\ \text{CI}\ [-5.14, -0.60]$, $\tau^2 = 2.25$), while `DCE-MRI` ($K^{\text{trans}}$) quantified progressive age-dependent BBB leakage in the cortex and hippocampus (Koizumi et al., 2018; Montagne et al., 2021; Laing et al., 2026). In rat ischemic stroke (`tifMCAO`; $n = 125$ studies), Mendes et al. (2026) demonstrated that while terminal `TTC` staining ($n = 51$ relative infarct studies) and wet/dry brain water assays ($n = 14$) remain common (Benedek et al., 2006; Türeyen et al., 2004), multimodal MRI sequences—specifically $T_2$-weighted imaging, `DWI/PWI` core–penumbra mapping, `ADC` mapping, and gadolinium-enhanced MRI—enable longitudinal tracking of cytotoxic-to-vasogenic edema transitions (up to $120\ \text{h}$) and biphasic BBB opening without terminal tissue destruction (Kuo et al., 2024; Mendes et al., 2026).
* **3D Optical Tissue Clearing & Ventral Root Tracing:** Combining retrograde ventral root labeling (alternating Fluorescein Dextran and Texas Red) with `ChAT` immunostaining and `RapiClear® 1.49` optical clearing, Sowoidnich et al. (2026) imaged intact murine lumbar spinal cords via 3D confocal microscopy, mapping a natural rostro–caudal motor neuron gradient rising from $\sim 300$ MNs in L1 to $\sim 700$ in L5 before declining to $\sim 300$ in L6 (Buettner et al., 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

### 3.3 AI-Driven Quantitative Image Analysis (Deep Learning Segmentation)
To overcome the labor intensity and inter-observer bias of manual stereological counting in 3D cleared organs, Sowoidnich et al. (2026) implemented an automated deep learning segmentation workflow using the open-source **Cellpose** framework (`TissueNet` base model fine-tuned via human-in-the-loop annotation) (Stringer et al., 2021; Pachitariu & Stringer, 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026):

* **Counting-Agreement Metric & Ground-Truth Concordance:** Applied to FIJI-preprocessed 3D confocal z-stacks of intact L1 spinal segments co-labeled for somatic `ChAT` and nuclear `HB9`, the trained **Cellpose** workflow was benchmarked against manual ground-truth counts using a study-defined adapted counting-agreement metric ($\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}$), achieving a concordance score of $0.86$ across both healthy controls and end-stage `SMNΔ7` mutants (Sowoidnich et al., 2026). As noted by the original authors, this adapted metric reflects object-level enumeration agreement between automated and manual counting rather than conventional machine-learning classification precision ($\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$) or pixel-wise segmentation accuracy.
* **Resolution of Disease-Specific Motor Pool Vulnerabilities:** Integrating unbiased **Cellpose** whole-mount counts with segment-specific `ChAT`/`NeuN` phenotyping resolved long-standing controversies across neurodegenerative mouse models:
  1. **Severe SMA (`SMNΔ7`):** Selective $\sim 30\text{–}50\%$ `p53`-mediated loss of $\alpha$-MNs (`ChAT+NeuN+`) innervating proximal and axial muscles (segments C6, T9, L1–L2, and the L5 medial motor column), while distal-innervating L3–L6 lateral motor columns remain spared (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
  2. **Mild Type III-like SMA (4-copy `SMN2`):** Complete preservation of $\alpha$- and $\gamma$-MNs, absence of nuclear `p53` activation, and fully innervated neuromuscular junctions across all spinal segments up to `P270`—overturning prior studies that reported $\sim 30\%$ late-onset MN loss based on non-specific Nissl/`SMI-32` staining (Bowerman et al., 2010; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).
  3. **ALS (`SOD1-G93A`):** Widespread $>50\%$ $\alpha$-MN degeneration and soma atrophy across all cervical, thoracic, and lumbar segments—most severe in distal-innervating L5 lateral motor columns—accompanied by complete sparing of $\gamma$-MNs (`ChAT+NeuN-`) (Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

## 4. Discussion & Translational Gaps

### 4.1 Synthesis of Multi-Scale Phenotyping Paradigms
A central insight emerging across these eight review-based publications and one original experimental study incorporating systematic review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation is that robust preclinical characterization of human disease requires bridging molecular signaling networks with spatially resolved histopathological, physiological, and three-dimensional (3D) morphometric readouts. In amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (`SOD1-G93A`), therapeutic modulation by electroacupuncture operates through a coordinated molecular cascade—suppressing `TLR4`/`NF-κB`/`p38` microglial neuroinflammation, activating `PI3K/Akt`–`p-GSK-3β` pro-survival signaling, inhibiting `RhoA`-mediated axonal growth cone collapse, and stabilizing `TDP-43` nucleocytoplasmic proteostasis—which ultimately manifests structurally as preserved motor neuron counts in the lumbar anterior horn (Jiang et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022, 2023; Lu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026). Similarly, in humanized `APOE4` mice, blood–brain barrier (BBB) destabilization does not arise from a single isolated defect, but from dynamic cross-talk between pericytic Cyclophilin A–`NF-κB`–`MMP9` enzymatic degradation of basement membrane collagen-IV, impaired `LRP1`–`PKCη`-mediated occludin phosphorylation, and maladaptive insulin/`mTOR` metabolic signaling (Bell et al., 2012; Nishitsuji et al., 2011; Yamazaki et al., 2020; Montagne et al., 2020, 2021; Laing et al., 2026).

Crucially, Sowoidnich et al. (2026) identified substantial variability (up to $60\%$) in reported motor neuron loss across spinal muscular atrophy (`SMA`) mouse studies, with anatomical sampling region emerging as a major methodological contributor. Their segment-specific analysis demonstrated that spinal cord sampling strategy, tissue processing, and marker selection profoundly influence estimated disease-associated neuronal loss: hydraulic spinal cord extrusion damages ventral roots and immersion fixation rapidly degrades cytoplasmic choline acetyltransferase (`ChAT`) immunoreactivity in adult myelinated tissue, whereas anterior corpectomy, transcardial perfusion, and `ChAT`/`HB9` immunolabeling prevent off-target misclassification of ventral horn interneurons (`V0`, `V2a/V2b`, `V3`, Renshaw cells, and ventral spinocerebellar tract neurons) that overlap in cross-sectional soma size with smaller $\alpha$- and $\gamma$-motor neurons (Borges & Iversen, 1986; Tsang et al., 2000; Ferrucci et al., 2018; Chalif et al., 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026). When paired with human-in-the-loop fine-tuned deep learning cellular segmentation (Stringer et al., 2021; Pachitariu & Stringer, 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026), this anatomically stratified framework clarifies that severe `SMNΔ7` mice exhibit `p53`-dependent loss of proximal/axial $\alpha$-motor neurons, mild Type III-like SMA mice retain fully intact motor pools and neuromuscular junctions, and `SOD1-G93A` ALS mice suffer pan-segmental $\alpha$-motor neuron degeneration (Bowerman et al., 2010; Sowoidnich et al., 2026).

Furthermore, in chemically and surgically induced disease models, pathological staging depends heavily on temporal and dosing kinetics rather than cumulative exposure alone. In rat colorectal carcinogenesis, high-dose/short-duration `DMH` regimens trigger early preneoplastic aberrant crypt foci (`ACF`), whereas prolonged lower-dose administration permits clonal expansion into invasive adenocarcinomas (Venkatachalam et al., 2020; Clapper et al., 2020; Salehi et al., 2026). Likewise, in rodent medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaws (`MRONJ`) models, histological osteonecrosis (Stage 0) precedes clinical bone exposure, necessitating a minimum 3- to 4-week post-extraction healing window to distinguish true pathological necrosis from normal socket remodeling (Biguetti et al., 2019; Aguirre et al., 2021; Yan et al., 2022; Maciel et al., 2026).

### 4.2 Translational Bottlenecks and Preclinical-to-Clinical Disconnects
While mouse models constitute the primary experimental focus across these investigations, integrating comparative rat, hamster, and human tissue validation data highlights four systemic disconnects that continue to limit clinical translation:

1. **Demographic Homogeneity vs. Clinical Comorbidity:** Sex and age distributions vary considerably across disease paradigms, yet several major preclinical domains exhibit marked demographic skew—evidenced by an $83.5\%$ male bias in oral epithelial dysplasia models (Ngokwe et al., 2026), $110$ male versus $6$ female studies in rat ischemic stroke (Mendes et al., 2026), and $77.7\%$ male Wistar rats in `DMH`-induced colorectal cancer (Salehi et al., 2026), whereas SMA and ALS murine cohorts typically balance sexes due to comparable motor neuron counts (Sowoidnich et al., 2026) and female `E4FAD` mice display heightened BBB fibrinogen extravasation (Barisano et al., 2022; Laing et al., 2026). Reliance on young, healthy, inbred cohorts in stroke and oncology fails to replicate aging clinical populations burdened by polypharmacy, metabolic comorbidities, and hormonal fluctuations that profoundly alter drug pharmacokinetics, inflammatory tone, and tissue repair (Sutherland et al., 2012; Dirnagl, 2006; Maciel et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026).
2. **Artificial Induction Mechanics & Anesthetic Confounding:** In rat `tifMCAO` stroke models, mechanical filament withdrawal induces abrupt reperfusion that diverges from gradual endogenous or thrombolytic clot lysis in human thromboembolic stroke, while routine isoflurane anesthesia introduces cerebral vasodilatory and neuroprotective confounding (Koizumi et al., 1986; Longa et al., 1989; Mendes et al., 2026). Similarly, artificial ovalbumin/aluminum hydroxide sensitization in BALB/c mice skews systemic Th2 responses differently than C57BL/6 mice or natural aeroallergen exposure in humans (He et al., 2015; Zeng & Zhou, 2023; Chen et al., 2026), and humanized anti-RANKL antibodies (denosumab) lack biological activity in wild-type rodents (Maciel et al., 2026).
3. **Interspecies Divergence & Control Genotype Selection:** While *Mus musculus* shares substantial genetic homology with humans (Vandamme, 2014; Maciel et al., 2026), species-specific differences in alveolar bone turnover, oral microbiota (Maciel et al., 2026), and inner ear vestibular penetrance (e.g., `PTPRQ` and `GRXCR1` knockout mice displaying severe circling and head-bobbing absent in human patients) require cautious cross-species extrapolation (Goodyear et al., 2012; Odeh et al., 2010; Ayoub et al., 2025). Moreover, in humanized transgenic lines, comparing `APOE4-TR` mice against non-humanized C57BL/6 wild-type mice rather than `APOE3-TR` littermates confounds isoform-specific mechanisms with allele-insertion artifacts (Lewandowski et al., 2020; Laing et al., 2026).
4. **Chronic Deficits in Reporting Transparency & Publication Bias:** Across nearly all synthesized reviews, SYRCLE risk-of-bias assessments (Hooijmans et al., 2014) revealed pervasive underreporting of sample size calculations, allocation concealment, random housing, and blinded outcome assessment (Hu et al., 2026; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026), compounded by significant publication bias in behavioral and inflammatory endpoints (Begg’s $p < 0.001$) that inflates perceived effect sizes (Macleod et al., 2004; Hu et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026).

### 4.3 Methodological Gaps and Future Directions
In the United States, evolving regulatory frameworks reflect a growing emphasis on integrating human-relevant models alongside refined animal studies: the **FDA Modernization Act 2.0** (enacted in 2022) expanded statutory recognition of non-animal testing methods, while the **FDA Modernization Act 3.0** (H.R. 2821), which passed the House in July 2026 and was subsequently received in the Senate, represents a further legislative effort to clarify regulatory implementation (Mirzayans & Murray, 2022; Ngokwe et al., 2026). Within this evolving landscape, future preclinical phenotyping workflows should prioritize four strategic advancements:

1. **Integration of 3D Patient-Derived Organoids (PDOs) & Longitudinal Multimodal Imaging:** In oral epithelial dysplasia, $88.9\%$ of models remain restricted to animal studies and $73.5\%$ of *in vitro* assays use 2D monolayers, with zero reported patient-derived organoid models (Ferreira et al., 2018; Abuwatfa et al., 2024; Ngokwe et al., 2026). Integrating 3D organoids and scaffold-based tissue constructs with longitudinal *in vivo* imaging ($T_2$/`DWI`/`ADC` MRI, `DCE-MRI` $K^{\text{trans}}$, $^{18}\text{F}$-`FDG-PET`, `micro-CT`, and optical coherence tomography) will capture dynamic stromal, vascular, and metabolic microenvironments without terminal sampling bias (Smith et al., 2011; Mian et al., 2017; Biguetti et al., 2019; Kuo et al., 2024; Onos et al., 2024; Laing et al., 2026; Maciel et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026).
2. **Comprehensive End-Organ & Multi-Omic Functional Annotation:** Large-scale murine phenotyping pipelines (such as the IMPC) should systematically integrate vestibular evoked potentials and balance assays alongside auditory brainstem responses, while expanding transcriptomic resolution across specific inner ear compartments (semicircular canals vs. otolith organs) and mitochondrial DNA variants (Dickinson et al., 2016; Muñoz-Fuentes et al., 2018; Ayoub et al., 2025).
3. **Scaling Deep Learning Image Segmentation Across Pathologies:** Extending open-source deep learning segmentation workflows from 3D cleared neuroanatomy (Stringer et al., 2021; Pachitariu & Stringer, 2022; Sowoidnich et al., 2026) to automated quantification of empty osteocyte lacunae in MRONJ (Maciel et al., 2026), mucosal eosinophil infiltration in allergic rhinitis (Chen et al., 2026), epithelial dysplasia grading (Ngokwe et al., 2026), and colonic `ACF` morphometry (Salehi et al., 2026) will eliminate inter-observer subjectivity and accelerate high-throughput histology.
4. **Enforcing ARRIVE 2.0, SYRCLE & Multicenter Preclinical Rigor:** Mandatory adoption of **ARRIVE 2.0** reporting checklists (Percie du Sert et al., 2020), *a priori* sample size calculations, sex-balanced cohorts, and collaborative multicenter preclinical trial networks—such as the Stroke Preclinical Assessment Network (`SPAN`), `MULTI-PART`, and `PRISM`—will substantially reduce single-laboratory bias and strengthen translational predictive validity (Fisher et al., 2009; Mendes et al., 2026; Salehi et al., 2026).

## 5. Conclusion
Deep morphological phenotyping bridges the critical divide between molecular genetics and translational medicine by resolving how cellular, vascular, and structural alterations evolve across intact tissues and organs. Synthesizing evidence across eight review-based publications and one original experimental study incorporating systematic review, meta-analysis, and methodological validation demonstrates that reliable preclinical phenotyping rests upon three interlocking pillars: (1) selecting biologically representative models and endpoint-tailored induction protocols—such as distinguishing Stage 0 histological osteonecrosis from macroscopic bone exposure in MRONJ (Maciel et al., 2026) or matching `DMH` dosing schedules to preneoplastic `ACF` versus invasive tumor endpoints (Salehi et al., 2026); (2) replacing non-specific histological stains and single-time-point terminal assays with lineage-specific molecular markers (`ChAT`, `HB9`, vascular extracellular matrix proteins) and longitudinal multimodal imaging (MRI, PET, 3D tissue clearing) (Laing et al., 2026; Mendes et al., 2026; Sowoidnich et al., 2026); and (3) deploying AI-driven automated cellular segmentation alongside strict adherence to **ARRIVE 2.0** and **SYRCLE** guidelines to eliminate observer bias and methodological heterogeneity. Together, these integrated strategies establish a rigorous, reproducible, and scalable paradigm for modern preclinical biomedical research.
