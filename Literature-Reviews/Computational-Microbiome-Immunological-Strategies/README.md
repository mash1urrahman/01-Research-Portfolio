# Literature Review: Emerging Computational, Microbiome-Based, and Immunological Strategies in Cancer Research and Molecular Diagnostics

---

## 1. Introduction
Recent developments in molecular biology, cancer immunology, microbiome research, and computational biology have created new opportunities for understanding disease mechanisms and improving therapeutic and diagnostic strategies. Cancer treatment, in particular, is increasingly influenced by the interaction between tumour cells, immune responses, host-associated microorganisms, molecular biomarkers, and computational approaches.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (**PD-1**) and programmed death-ligand 1 (**PD-L1**) have transformed cancer immunotherapy. However, therapeutic resistance and variable patient responses remain major challenges. At the same time, microbiome research has expanded beyond infectious disease and antimicrobial resistance (**AMR**) into cancer immunotherapy, tumour microenvironment modulation, and biomarker discovery.

Parallel advances in artificial intelligence (**AI**), multi-omics, metagenomics, whole-genome sequencing (**WGS**), and molecular docking have strengthened the ability to investigate complex biological systems. These approaches can support the identification of disease-associated molecular patterns, prediction of antimicrobial resistance, characterization of protein function, and development of clinically relevant diagnostic frameworks.

This review synthesizes **15 selected research papers** covering cancer immunotherapy, immune checkpoint resistance, microbiome engineering, AI-assisted diagnostics, antimicrobial resistance genomics, structural bioinformatics, and molecular docking. The objective is to identify major research directions, methodological connections, limitations, and opportunities for further investigation from the perspective of molecular and computational biosciences.

## 2. Cancer Immunology and Immune Checkpoint Resistance
Immune checkpoint blockade has become an important therapeutic strategy in cancer treatment. Nevertheless, not all patients respond to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, and resistance may emerge through multiple biological mechanisms.

* ***Immune-pressure redistribution in resistance to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade: mechanisms, biomarkers, and therapeutic design*** — Examines the mechanisms through which immune pressure may be redistributed during resistance to immune checkpoint blockade. Its focus on resistance mechanisms, biomarkers, and therapeutic design highlights the importance of understanding tumour–immune interactions beyond the expression of a single checkpoint molecule.
* ***Natural polysaccharides as immune adjuvants to overcome immune checkpoint inhibitor resistance: mechanistic insights, clinical evidence, and translational challenges*** — Explores natural polysaccharides as potential immune adjuvants and discusses their possible role in modifying immune responses and improving checkpoint inhibitor efficacy, connecting immunological signalling, tumour microenvironment regulation, and translational therapeutic development.

Together, these papers emphasize that immune checkpoint resistance is a complex biological phenomenon. A more complete understanding requires investigation of tumour-associated immune populations, molecular signalling pathways, immunoregulatory factors, and potential therapeutic combinations.

## 3. Microbiome–Immune Interactions and Cancer Therapy
The relationship between host-associated microorganisms and cancer immunity has become an important area of translational research. Microbial communities may influence immune regulation, inflammatory signalling, metabolite production, and responses to cancer treatment.

* ***Innovative approaches in the treatment of hematologic malignancies: the role of CRISPR-engineered microbiomes along the gut-immune axis in immunotherapy development*** — Connects microbiome engineering with immunotherapy for hematologic malignancies, highlighting CRISPR-engineered microbial systems and the gut–immune axis for innovative blood cancer therapeutics.
* ***The application of AI-driven and engineered intratumoral microbes in cancer therapy*** — Extends microbiome engineering to the tumour microenvironment, illustrating how engineered intratumoral microorganisms and computational approaches offer opportunities for targeted therapeutic delivery and local tumour modulation.
* ***Microbiome Modulation in Lung Cancer Immunotherapy: Unveiling the Role of Respiratory and Gut Microbiota in the PD-1/PD-L1 Response*** — Focuses on the contribution of respiratory and gut microbiota to immune checkpoint inhibitor responses in lung cancer.
* ***Design and application of synthetic human gut microbial communities*** — Provides a broader foundation for how defined microbial communities can be designed and studied under controlled conditions to investigate host–microbe relationships and functional microbial contributions.

Collectively, these studies indicate that microbiome research is moving from observational profiling toward functional manipulation and engineered therapeutic systems. However, clinical translation requires careful validation of microbial safety, stability, reproducibility, host interactions, and therapeutic effectiveness.

## 4. AI, Multi-Omics, and Cancer Biomarker Discovery
The integration of computational methods with molecular data is increasingly important for cancer diagnosis and precision medicine.

* ***Defining a Multi-Omic, AI-Enabled Stool Screening Paradigm for Colorectal Cancer: A Consensus Framework for Clinical Translation*** — Presents a consensus framework connecting stool-based screening, multi-omics, and AI-assisted clinical translation for non-invasive biomarker discovery.
* ***Artificial intelligence and oral microbiome: Reshaping the diagnostic and therapeutic paradigm of OSCC*** — Explores the intersection of AI, oral microbiome research, and oral squamous cell carcinoma (**OSCC**), demonstrating how microbial profiles and computational classification support cancer detection and therapeutic decision-making.

These studies highlight the potential of combining molecular biomarkers with computational interpretation. Multi-omics integration improves the characterization of disease-associated biological patterns, while AI-based approaches help identify complex relationships difficult to detect through conventional analysis alone. However, clinical utility depends on training data quality and diversity, independent validation, reproducibility, interpretability, and cross-population evaluation.

## 5. Metagenomics, Whole-Genome Sequencing, and Antimicrobial Resistance
Antimicrobial resistance is a major global health challenge, and genomic approaches have become essential for understanding resistance mechanisms and tracking resistance-associated genetic elements.

* ***Metagenomics for antimicrobial resistance: from resistome surveillance to mechanistic inference*** — Discusses the application of metagenomics to resistome analysis and mechanistic inference without requiring the isolation of every organism.
* ***The role of whole genome sequencing in antimicrobial susceptibility prediction of bacteria: 2025 update from the European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing Subcommittee*** — Evaluates the role of **WGS** in predicting antimicrobial susceptibility by identifying resistance-associated mutations and genes within validated interpretive frameworks.
* ***Artificial intelligence in microbiology: implications for metagenomics, diagnostics, and AMR surveillance*** — Connects computational methods with microbiological data analysis, metagenomics, diagnostics, and resistance surveillance across large, complex datasets.
* ***Advances in integrated antimicrobial resistance diagnostics: quantitative, qualitative and AI-driven approaches*** — Examines the integration of quantitative, qualitative, and AI-driven molecular diagnostic strategies to support informed clinical decisions.

These papers demonstrate how genomics, metagenomics, molecular diagnostics, and computational analysis complement one another, while highlighting key challenges such as incomplete reference databases, variation in resistance mechanisms, data standardization, and genotype-to-phenotype concordance.

## 6. Structural Bioinformatics and Molecular Docking
Structural bioinformatics provides a computational framework for investigating protein structure, molecular interactions, and potential therapeutic targets.

* ***Structural and Kinetic Variability of Pathogen Cyclophilins: Functional Diversification and Therapeutic Potential*** — Focuses on structural and functional variation in pathogen cyclophilins, linking protein architecture, kinetic behaviour, and ligand interactions to therapeutic target identification.
* ***Molecular docking approaches in mycetoma: Toward improved patient management*** — Highlights disease-focused molecular docking to investigate candidate compound–target protein interactions as an initial computational basis for experimental validation.

While these directions connect directly to structural analysis and computational drug discovery, docking scores alone cannot establish biological activity or therapeutic efficacy; reliable interpretation requires target validation, structural assessment, biochemical confirmation, and complementary molecular dynamics simulations.

## 7. Integrated Research Themes
Across the 15 selected papers, five major research themes emerge:

1. **Cancer immunology and therapeutic resistance:** Understanding immune checkpoint resistance and developing strategies to improve immunotherapy.
2. **Microbiome engineering and host–microbe interactions:** Investigating how microbial communities influence immune regulation and cancer treatment.
3. **AI-assisted molecular diagnostics:** Integrating multi-omics, microbial data, and computational methods for biomarker discovery and disease classification.
4. **Genomics and antimicrobial resistance:** Using metagenomics and WGS to characterize resistance-associated genetic patterns and improve diagnostic interpretation.
5. **Structural bioinformatics and molecular interaction analysis:** Applying computational structural methods to investigate protein function and potential therapeutic interactions.

An important connection across these themes is the growing need to integrate experimental molecular biology with computational analysis across genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, microbial, and structural datasets.

---

## 8. Research Gaps and Future Directions
* **8.1 Mechanistic validation:** Computational predictions and biomarker associations require functional assays and biologically relevant experimental models.
* **8.2 Integration of multi-omics datasets:** Combining genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and microbiome data requires overcoming differences in data quality, sample size, and analytical pipelines.
* **8.3 Clinical validation of AI-based methods:** AI-assisted diagnostic systems require external validation across independent cohorts alongside interpretability and reproducibility.
* **8.4 Microbiome-based therapeutic translation:** Engineered and synthetic microbial communities require rigorous evaluation of safety, stability, host compatibility, and reproducible efficacy.
* **8.5 Linking structural predictions with biological function:** Molecular docking and structural modeling should be paired with molecular dynamics and biochemical validation.
* **8.6 Translational relevance in cancer research:** Further studies are needed to connect molecular biomarkers, immune responses, microbiome composition, and clinical treatment outcomes.

---

## 9. Conclusion
The selected literature illustrates the expanding role of molecular biology, cancer immunology, microbiome science, genomics, and computational biology in modern biomedical research. Research on immune checkpoint resistance and microbiome-based immunotherapy demonstrates the importance of understanding complex interactions between tumour cells, immune systems, and microbial communities. Simultaneously, AI-enabled multi-omics, metagenomics, WGS, structural bioinformatics, and molecular docking offer complementary tools for investigating disease mechanisms and advancing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Future progress will depend on reproducible analytical pipelines, mechanistic validation, reliable datasets, and carefully designed translational studies.

## 10. Scope and Limitation of This Review
This document is an abstract-based narrative synthesis of **15 selected papers**, prepared as a research portfolio literature review and an initial framework for academic investigation. It should not be interpreted as a systematic review or a complete evaluation of full-text methodologies across all included studies. Full-text examination and verified bibliographic citations are required prior to formal manuscript submission or journal publication.
